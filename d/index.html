D chords

Every chord built on the note D: twelve types, each on guitar, ukulele and keyboard.

About chords on D

All the chords on this page share the root D; they differ in the notes added above it. The major chord takes the third and fifth notes of the D major scale, whose notes are, by the names used on this site, D, E, F♯, G, A, B, C♯. Lower the third and the chord is minor; add a seventh, sixth or ninth for the four-note chords; replace the third with the second or fourth for the suspended chords. The formulas are on how chords are built.

The key of D major

In the key of D major, the chords that belong together are: D (I), Em (ii), F♯m (iii), G (IV), A (V), Bm (vi), C♯dim (vii°). The first, fourth and fifth are the main chords; start with those three and add the sixth, Bm, for contrast. The same seven chords, starting from Bm, make the key of B minor.

Where to start

Learn D and Dm first, then D7. On guitar, look at the first chart on each page: it is the easiest shape. Where the first shape is a barre chord and that is still hard for you, the page for the power chord gives a two-finger stand-in, and a capo can move easier shapes to this root: see capo and transposing.
