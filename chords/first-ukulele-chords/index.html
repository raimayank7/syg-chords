Your first ukulele chords

The ukulele is the kindest instrument for a beginner. It has four nylon strings that are soft on the fingers, small frets and several chords that need only one finger. With the first four chords on this page you can accompany a surprising number of songs, and you can learn them in an afternoon.

Getting ready

  • Tune to G, C, E, A with our neighbour site Tuner. Nylon strings drift, so tune every time you play.
  • Hold the ukulele against your chest with the right forearm, the neck pointing slightly upwards. The left hand should not have to hold the instrument up.
  • Strum with the pad of the thumb or the nail side of the index finger, over the place where the neck meets the body.
  • Read how to read chord diagrams. On a ukulele diagram the four lines are the G, C, E and A strings from left to right, and the string nearest your face when playing is the G.

The first four

  • C major: one finger, the ring finger, on the 3rd fret of the A string (the one nearest the floor). The other three strings are open.
  • A minor: one finger, the middle finger, on the 2nd fret of the G string. Everything else open.
  • F major: A minor plus the index finger on the 1st fret of the E string. Keep both fingers arched so that the open C and A strings ring.
  • G major: a small triangle of three fingers. It is the first real challenge; place the fingers one at a time at first, then all together.

These four belong to the key of C. In the language of keys they are chords I, vi, IV and V, the most used four chords in popular music.

The next four

  • G7: the G triangle turned upside down. It pulls strongly back to C.
  • D minor: three fingers; combine with F and A minor for a gentle minor sound.
  • E minor: a diagonal staircase on three strings.
  • D major: three fingers in a row at the 2nd fret; many players use one flattened finger and let it miss the A string.

Practising changes

  1. C to A minor: lift one finger, place another. Do it twenty times.
  2. A minor to F: add one finger. The middle finger never moves.
  3. F to C, then C to G, then G back to C.
  4. Play all four in a loop: C, A minor, F, G, four strums each, slowly, without stopping. If a chord is late, keep strumming and catch up; an unbroken rhythm matters more than a perfect chord.

When that is comfortable, speed up a little each day. More ideas are on changing chords smoothly.

A first strumming pattern

Count "one, two, three, four" and strum down on each count. Then add an up-strum between the counts: down, down-up, down, down-up. Keep the hand swinging like a pendulum even on the strokes you leave out.

Common troubles

  • A string sounds dead: a finger is leaning on it. Arch the fingers and use the tips.
  • Buzzing: press closer to the fret.
  • Sore fingertips: normal for the first week, and much milder than on a guitar.
  • Everything sounds wrong: retune. New strings stretch for days.

Where the ukulele differs from the guitar

If you already play guitar: ukulele shapes are the shapes of the guitar's four thin strings, but they sound a fourth higher. The shape you know as D on a guitar is G on a ukulele. The 4th string is also tuned high, so chords have a close, bright sound with no deep bass.
