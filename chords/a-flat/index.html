A♭ chords

Every chord built on the note A♭: twelve types, each on guitar, ukulele and keyboard. A♭ is the same note as G♯, so these are also the G♯ chords.

About chords on A♭

All the chords on this page share the root A♭; they differ in the notes added above it. The major chord takes the third and fifth notes of the A♭ major scale, whose notes are, by the names used on this site, A♭, B♭, C, C♯, E♭, F, G. Lower the third and the chord is minor; add a seventh, sixth or ninth for the four-note chords; replace the third with the second or fourth for the suspended chords. The formulas are on how chords are built.

The key of A♭ major

In the key of A♭ major, the chords that belong together are: A♭ (I), B♭m (ii), Cm (iii), C♯ (IV), E♭ (V), Fm (vi), Gdim (vii°). The first, fourth and fifth are the main chords; start with those three and add the sixth, Fm, for contrast. The same seven chords, starting from Fm, make the key of F minor.

Where to start

Learn A♭ and A♭m first, then A♭7. On guitar, look at the first chart on each page: it is the easiest shape. Where the first shape is a barre chord and that is still hard for you, the page for the power chord gives a two-finger stand-in, and a capo can move easier shapes to this root: see capo and transposing.
