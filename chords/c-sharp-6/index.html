C♯6 chord (C♯ sixth)

The C♯ sixth chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
C♯6
Notes
C♯, E♯, G♯, A♯
Formula
1 3 5 6
Semitones from root
0, 4, 7, 9

A sixth chord, written C♯6, is a major chord with the sixth note of the scale added, a whole step above the fifth. Its notes are the root, major third, perfect fifth and major sixth. It is the same chord as D♭6; the two names are used in different keys.

It sounds sweet and relaxed, with an old-fashioned charm, and is common at the end of swing and early film songs. It contains the same notes as the minor seventh chord built on its sixth note: C6 and A minor seventh are the same four notes with a different bass.

C♯6 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 5 (A): fret 4. String 4 (D): fret 6. String 3 (G): fret 6. String 2 (B): fret 6. String 1 (E): fret 6. Do not play string 6.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 6th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 9 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 11. String 4 (D): fret 11. String 3 (G): fret 10. String 2 (B): fret 11. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

In some compact guitar shapes the fifth is omitted. The sixth is often just the major chord with one more string fretted or opened.

C♯6 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): Lay your first finger flat across strings 4 to 1 at fret 1 (a barre).

C♯6 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: C♯, E♯, G♯, A♯. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: E♯, G♯, A♯, C♯; the second inversion: G♯, A♯, C♯, E♯; the third inversion: A♯, C♯, E♯, G♯. All are still C♯6.

Play the major chord and add the note a whole step above the fifth. To hear its double nature, hold the chord and play the sixth in the bass instead of the root.

Where C♯6 fits

The plain chord on this root, C♯ major, is the home chord (I) of the key of C♯ major, the IV chord of A♭ major and the V chord of F♯ major. C♯6 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of C♯ major, the three main chords are C♯, F♯ and A♭, and the most used minor chord is B♭m. Try the movement C♯6, F♯, A♭, C♯6.
