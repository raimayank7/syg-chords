Baug chord (B augmented)

The B augmented chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
Baug
Notes
B, D♯, G
Formula
1 3 ♯5
Semitones from root
0, 4, 8

An augmented chord, written Baug or with a plus sign, is a major chord with the fifth raised by a semitone. Its notes are the root, the major third and the augmented fifth, each four semitones from the next.

It sounds strange, floating and expectant. Because its notes divide the octave into three equal parts, any of them can serve as the root: C, E and A flat augmented all contain the same three notes. It is usually a passing chord, the raised fifth climbing on to a note of the next chord.

Baug on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 5 (A): fret 2. String 4 (D): fret 5. String 3 (G): fret 4. String 2 (B): fret 4. String 1 (E): fret 3. Do not play string 6.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 6th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 7 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 10. String 4 (D): fret 9. String 3 (G): fret 8. String 2 (B): fret 8. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

The shape repeats every four frets, a consequence of its symmetry. Move from the major chord to the augmented chord by raising one note one fret.

Baug on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 3 (C): fret 3. String 2 (E): fret 3. String 1 (A): fret 2. Play string 4 open. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

Baug on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: B, D♯, G. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: D♯, G, B; the second inversion: G, B, D♯. All are still Baug.

Play the major chord and raise the top note a semitone. Then move that note up one more semitone to reach the sixth chord: major, augmented, sixth is a classic rising line.

Where Baug fits

The plain chord on this root, B major, is the home chord (I) of the key of B major, the IV chord of F♯ major and the V chord of E major. Baug is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of B major, the three main chords are B, E and F♯, and the most used minor chord is A♭m. Try the movement Baug, E, F♯, Baug.
