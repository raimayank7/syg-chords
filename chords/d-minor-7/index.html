Dm7 chord (D minor seventh)

The D minor seventh chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
Dm7
Notes
D, F, A, C
Formula
1 ♭3 5 ♭7
Semitones from root
0, 3, 7, 10

A minor seventh chord, written Dm7, adds the minor seventh to a minor chord. Its four notes are the root, minor third, perfect fifth and minor seventh.

It is smoother and less sad than a plain minor chord, mellow more than mournful. It appears on the second, third and sixth chords of a major key, and the movement from a minor seventh to a dominant seventh to a major chord (two, five, one) is the basic sentence of jazz harmony.

Dm7 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 3 (G): fret 2. String 2 (B): fret 1. String 1 (E): fret 1. Play string 4 open. Do not play strings 6, 5.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 5th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 5 to 1 at fret 5 (a barre). String 4 (D): fret 7. String 2 (B): fret 6. Do not play string 6. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Many minor seventh shapes are simply the minor chord with one finger lifted. They are forgiving chords for beginners and sound fuller than the effort suggests.

Dm7 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 2. String 3 (C): fret 2. String 2 (E): fret 1. String 1 (A): fret 3.

Dm7 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: D, F, A, C. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: F, A, C, D; the second inversion: A, C, D, F; the third inversion: C, D, F, A. All are still Dm7.

Notice that the top three notes of this chord form a major chord: for example, the upper notes of A minor seventh are C, E and G. Thinking of it as "a major chord over a different bass note" makes it easy to find.

Where Dm7 fits

D minor is the home chord of the key of D minor. It also belongs to three major keys: it is the ii chord of C major, the iii chord of B♭ major and the vi chord of F major. Dm7 can replace it in any of those places.

In D minor it is usually joined by Gm and Am, and by the major chords F, B♭ and C. Try Dm7, B♭, F, C.
