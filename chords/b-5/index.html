B5 chord (B power chord (fifth))

The B power chord (fifth) chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
B5
Notes
B, F♯
Formula
1 5
Semitones from root
0, 7

A power chord, written B5, has only two different notes: the root and the perfect fifth, usually with the root repeated an octave higher. Strictly it is an interval, not a chord, since a chord needs three notes.

Without a third it is neither major nor minor, so it fits over either. On an electric guitar with distortion it sounds clear and strong where a full chord would turn to mud, which made it the foundation of rock, punk and metal. On an acoustic guitar it gives a lean, driving accompaniment.

B5 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 5 (A): fret 2. String 4 (D): fret 4. String 3 (G): fret 4. Do not play strings 6, 2, 1.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 6th string. String 6 (E): fret 7. String 5 (A): fret 9. String 4 (D): fret 9. Do not play strings 3, 2, 1. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Play only the two or three thickest strings of the shape and mute the rest with the side of your fretting hand. The shape slides up and down the neck unchanged: learn it once and you have every power chord.

B5 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): Lay your first finger flat across strings 2 to 1 at fret 2 (a barre). String 4 (G): fret 4. String 3 (C): fret 6. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

B5 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: B, F♯. With only two different notes, the root is usually doubled an octave higher.

Play root and fifth in the left hand, or root, fifth and octave. This "open fifth" is the usual left-hand bass under right-hand chords.

Where B5 fits

The plain chord on this root, B major, is the home chord (I) of the key of B major, the IV chord of F♯ major and the V chord of E major. B5 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of B major, the three main chords are B, E and F♯, and the most used minor chord is A♭m. Try the movement B5, E, F♯, B5.
