E♭7 chord (E♭ dominant seventh)

The E♭ dominant seventh chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
E♭7
Notes
E♭, G, B♭, D♭
Formula
1 3 5 ♭7
Semitones from root
0, 4, 7, 10

A dominant seventh chord, written E♭7, is a major chord with a fourth note added: the minor seventh, a whole step below the root's octave. Its four notes are the root, major third, perfect fifth and minor seventh. It is the same chord as D♯7; the two names are used in different keys.

It sounds restless, as if it needs to move on. Its natural move is to the chord a fourth above (five semitones up): this pull from the "five" chord to the "one" chord is the strongest in Western harmony, and it drives blues, where every chord may be a seventh, as well as classical cadences and qawwali harmonium playing.

E♭7 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 4 (D): fret 1. String 3 (G): fret 3. String 2 (B): fret 2. String 1 (E): fret 3. Do not play strings 6, 5.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 5th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 5 to 1 at fret 6 (a barre). String 4 (D): fret 8. String 2 (B): fret 8. Do not play string 6. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Seventh chords are often easier than the major chords they come from, because the added note is made by lifting a finger or adding one on a thin string. In many common shapes the fifth of the chord is left out; the chord keeps its character without it.

E♭7 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): Lay your first finger flat across strings 4 to 1 at fret 3 (a barre). String 1 (A): fret 4.

E♭7 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: E♭, G, B♭, D♭. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: G, B♭, D♭, E♭; the second inversion: B♭, D♭, E♭, G; the third inversion: D♭, E♭, G, B♭. All are still E♭7.

With four notes, use fingers 1, 2, 3 and 5. If the stretch is wide, leave out the fifth: root, third and seventh carry the sound.

Where E♭7 fits

E♭7 is the V7 chord of the key of A♭ major and of A♭ minor: it leads home to A♭ or A♭m. Play E♭7 and then A♭ to hear the pull. In a twelve-bar blues in E♭, the three chords are E♭7, A♭7 and B♭7.
