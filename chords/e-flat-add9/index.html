E♭add9 chord (E♭ added ninth)

The E♭ added ninth chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
E♭add9
Notes
E♭, G, B♭, F
Formula
1 3 5 9
Semitones from root
0, 4, 7, 2

An added ninth chord, written E♭add9, is a major chord with the ninth added: the same note as the second of the scale, an octave higher. Its notes are the root, major third, perfect fifth and ninth. Unlike a full ninth chord, it has no seventh. It is the same chord as D♯add9; the two names are used in different keys.

It keeps the brightness of the major chord and adds a shimmer. It is a favourite of acoustic guitarists and of modern pop and worship music, wherever a plain major chord would sound too square.

E♭add9 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): Lay your first finger flat across strings 5 to 1 at fret 6 (a barre). String 4 (D): fret 8. String 3 (G): fret 10. String 2 (B): fret 8. Do not play string 6.

Some shapes need a stretch; others are easier than the plain major chord. Let all the strings ring: the ninth should sound against the third.

E♭add9 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 3 (C): fret 3. String 2 (E): fret 1. String 1 (A): fret 1. Play string 4 open.

E♭add9 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: E♭, G, B♭, F. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: G, B♭, F, E♭; the second inversion: B♭, F, E♭, G; the third inversion: F, E♭, G, B♭. All are still E♭add9.

Play the major chord with the right hand and add the note a whole step above the root, either between root and third or at the top. Putting it at the top is clearer.

Where E♭add9 fits

The plain chord on this root, E♭ major, is the home chord (I) of the key of E♭ major, the IV chord of B♭ major and the V chord of A♭ major. E♭add9 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of E♭ major, the three main chords are E♭, A♭ and B♭, and the most used minor chord is Cm. Try the movement E♭add9, A♭, B♭, E♭add9.
