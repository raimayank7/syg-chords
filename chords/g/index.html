G chords

Every chord built on the note G: twelve types, each on guitar, ukulele and keyboard.

About chords on G

All the chords on this page share the root G; they differ in the notes added above it. The major chord takes the third and fifth notes of the G major scale, whose notes are, by the names used on this site, G, A, B, C, D, E, F♯. Lower the third and the chord is minor; add a seventh, sixth or ninth for the four-note chords; replace the third with the second or fourth for the suspended chords. The formulas are on how chords are built.

The key of G major

In the key of G major, the chords that belong together are: G (I), Am (ii), Bm (iii), C (IV), D (V), Em (vi), F♯dim (vii°). The first, fourth and fifth are the main chords; start with those three and add the sixth, Em, for contrast. The same seven chords, starting from Em, make the key of E minor.

Where to start

Learn G and Gm first, then G7. On guitar, look at the first chart on each page: it is the easiest shape. Where the first shape is a barre chord and that is still hard for you, the page for the power chord gives a two-finger stand-in, and a capo can move easier shapes to this root: see capo and transposing.
