How to read chord diagrams

A chord diagram, also called a chord box or chord chart, is a small picture of the neck of a guitar or ukulele that shows where to put your fingers. Once you can read one, you can play any chord on this site, or in any song book, without reading music. It takes five minutes to learn.

The picture

Imagine the instrument standing upright in front of you, with the headstock at the top and the strings running down.

  • Vertical lines are strings. On a guitar diagram the line on the far left is the 6th string, the thickest (low E), and the line on the far right is the 1st string, the thinnest (high E). On a ukulele the four lines are, left to right, the G, C, E and A strings.
  • Horizontal lines are frets. The thick line at the top is the nut, the end of the fretboard. The space below it is the 1st fret, the next space the 2nd, and so on.
  • A dot means: press this string in this fret space, just behind the metal fret.
  • O above a string means: play this string open, without pressing it.
  • X above a string means: do not play this string. Either avoid it with your strumming hand or touch it lightly so that it does not ring.
  • A bar across several strings is a barre: one finger, usually the first, lies flat across all those strings at that fret.
  • A number beside the box, such as "5fr", means the diagram starts higher up the neck: the top line is then not the nut, and the first space shown is that fret.

The short code

Under each diagram we also give the chord as a row of numbers, for example x 3 2 0 1 0. Read it from the thickest string to the thinnest: x is a string you do not play, 0 is an open string, and any other number is the fret to press. The example is C major: 6th string not played, 5th string 3rd fret, 4th string 2nd fret, 3rd string open, 2nd string 1st fret, 1st string open.

Which finger?

Fingers of the fretting hand are numbered: 1 is the index finger, 2 the middle, 3 the ring finger and 4 the little finger. The thumb stays behind the neck. Our diagrams show where the notes are and the text describes any barre; for the remaining dots, the usual rule is one finger per fret: the dot nearest the nut gets finger 1, the next fret finger 2, and so on. Where two dots share a fret, the finger on the thicker string is the lower-numbered one.

Checking your chord

  1. Place the fingers, with the fingertips upright, close behind the frets.
  2. Pick each string of the chord one at a time, from thickest to thinnest.
  3. If a string buzzes, move the finger closer to the fret or press a little harder.
  4. If a string is silent, a neighbouring finger is touching it: arch that finger more.
  5. Then strum only the strings in the chord.
  6. Tap "Hear this chord" on any chord page to compare.

Reading the keyboard diagram

For piano and keyboard, each chord page shows two octaves of keys with the chord's notes marked. The diagram shows the chord in root position, that is, with the root as the lowest note. You may play the same notes in any order and in any octave; rearrangements are called inversions and are explained on piano chords and inversions.

Left-handed players

If you play a left-handed guitar strung in reverse, read each diagram as its mirror image: the thickest string is then on the right of the picture.
