F♯ chords

Every chord built on the note F♯: twelve types, each on guitar, ukulele and keyboard. F♯ is the same note as G♭, so these are also the G♭ chords.

About chords on F♯

All the chords on this page share the root F♯; they differ in the notes added above it. The major chord takes the third and fifth notes of the F♯ major scale, whose notes are, by the names used on this site, F♯, A♭, B♭, B, C♯, E♭, F. Lower the third and the chord is minor; add a seventh, sixth or ninth for the four-note chords; replace the third with the second or fourth for the suspended chords. The formulas are on how chords are built.

The key of F♯ major

In the key of F♯ major, the chords that belong together are: F♯ (I), A♭m (ii), B♭m (iii), B (IV), C♯ (V), E♭m (vi), Fdim (vii°). The first, fourth and fifth are the main chords; start with those three and add the sixth, E♭m, for contrast. The same seven chords, starting from E♭m, make the key of E♭ minor.

Where to start

Learn F♯ and F♯m first, then F♯7. On guitar, look at the first chart on each page: it is the easiest shape. Where the first shape is a barre chord and that is still hard for you, the page for the power chord gives a two-finger stand-in, and a capo can move easier shapes to this root: see capo and transposing.
