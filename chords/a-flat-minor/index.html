A♭m chord (A♭ minor)

The A♭ minor chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
A♭m
Notes
A♭, C♭, E♭
Formula
1 ♭3 5
Semitones from root
0, 3, 7

A minor chord is a major chord with its middle note lowered by one semitone. Its three notes are the root, the minor third and the perfect fifth. It is written with a small m after the letter: A♭m. It is the same chord as G♯m; the two names are used in different keys.

That one lowered note changes the mood completely: minor chords sound darker, sadder or more serious than major ones. Songs in minor keys are built round them, and almost every song in a major key borrows two or three for contrast.

A♭m on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 4 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 6. String 4 (D): fret 6.

A second way: movable shape on the four thin strings. String 4 (D): fret 6. String 3 (G): fret 8. String 2 (B): fret 9. String 1 (E): fret 7. Do not play strings 6, 5. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Compare this shape with the major chord on the same root: usually only one finger moves, by one fret. Practise switching between the two to hear the third change.

A♭m on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 1. String 3 (C): fret 3. String 2 (E): fret 4. String 1 (A): fret 2. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

A♭m on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: A♭, C♭, E♭. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: C♭, E♭, A♭; the second inversion: E♭, A♭, C♭. All are still A♭m.

Play root, minor third and fifth with fingers 1, 3 and 5. Play the major chord, then lower only the middle note: that is the whole difference.

Where A♭m fits

A♭ minor is the home chord of the key of A♭ minor. It also belongs to three major keys: it is the ii chord of F♯ major, the iii chord of E major and the vi chord of B major.

In A♭ minor it is usually joined by C♯m and E♭m, and by the major chords B, E and F♯. Try A♭m, E, B, F♯.
