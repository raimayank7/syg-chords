Aaug chord (A augmented)

The A augmented chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
Aaug
Notes
A, C♯, E♯
Formula
1 3 ♯5
Semitones from root
0, 4, 8

An augmented chord, written Aaug or with a plus sign, is a major chord with the fifth raised by a semitone. Its notes are the root, the major third and the augmented fifth, each four semitones from the next.

It sounds strange, floating and expectant. Because its notes divide the octave into three equal parts, any of them can serve as the root: C, E and A flat augmented all contain the same three notes. It is usually a passing chord, the raised fifth climbing on to a note of the next chord.

Aaug on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 4 (D): fret 3. String 3 (G): fret 2. String 2 (B): fret 2. String 1 (E): fret 1. Play string 5 open. Do not play string 6.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 6th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 5 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 8. String 4 (D): fret 7. String 3 (G): fret 6. String 2 (B): fret 6. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

The shape repeats every four frets, a consequence of its symmetry. Move from the major chord to the augmented chord by raising one note one fret.

Aaug on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 2. String 3 (C): fret 1. String 2 (E): fret 1. Play string 1 open. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

Aaug on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: A, C♯, E♯. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: C♯, E♯, A; the second inversion: E♯, A, C♯. All are still Aaug.

Play the major chord and raise the top note a semitone. Then move that note up one more semitone to reach the sixth chord: major, augmented, sixth is a classic rising line.

Where Aaug fits

The plain chord on this root, A major, is the home chord (I) of the key of A major, the IV chord of E major and the V chord of D major. Aaug is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of A major, the three main chords are A, D and E, and the most used minor chord is F♯m. Try the movement Aaug, D, E, Aaug.
