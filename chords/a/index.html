A chords

Every chord built on the note A: twelve types, each on guitar, ukulele and keyboard.

About chords on A

All the chords on this page share the root A; they differ in the notes added above it. The major chord takes the third and fifth notes of the A major scale, whose notes are, by the names used on this site, A, B, C♯, D, E, F♯, A♭. Lower the third and the chord is minor; add a seventh, sixth or ninth for the four-note chords; replace the third with the second or fourth for the suspended chords. The formulas are on how chords are built.

The key of A major

In the key of A major, the chords that belong together are: A (I), Bm (ii), C♯m (iii), D (IV), E (V), F♯m (vi), A♭dim (vii°). The first, fourth and fifth are the main chords; start with those three and add the sixth, F♯m, for contrast. The same seven chords, starting from F♯m, make the key of F♯ minor.

Where to start

Learn A and Am first, then A7. On guitar, look at the first chart on each page: it is the easiest shape. Where the first shape is a barre chord and that is still hard for you, the page for the power chord gives a two-finger stand-in, and a capo can move easier shapes to this root: see capo and transposing.
