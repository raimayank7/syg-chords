Asus2 chord (A suspended second)

The A suspended second chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
Asus2
Notes
A, B, E
Formula
1 2 5
Semitones from root
0, 2, 7

A suspended second chord, written Asus2, replaces the third of the chord with the second note of the scale, a whole step above the root. Its notes are the root, the major second and the perfect fifth.

With no third, the chord is neither major nor minor. It sounds open, airy and unresolved, and it often slides to the ordinary major or minor chord on the same root, or back again, as a decoration. Guitarists use it constantly to add movement to a held chord.

Asus2 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 4 (D): fret 2. String 3 (G): fret 2. Play strings 5, 2, 1 open. Do not play string 6.

A second way: movable shape on the four thin strings. Lay your first finger flat across strings 4 to 1 at fret 7 (a barre). String 3 (G): fret 9. String 2 (B): fret 10. Do not play strings 6, 5. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

From an open major chord, a sus2 is usually made by lifting one finger. Practise the pattern major, sus2, major in rhythm: it is the sound of countless acoustic guitar parts.

Asus2 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 4. String 3 (C): fret 4. Play strings 2, 1 open. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

Asus2 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: A, B, E. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: B, E, A; the second inversion: E, A, B. All are still Asus2.

Play root, second and fifth, then move the second up to the third to "resolve" the chord. Hearing the suspended note fall into place is the point of the chord.

Where Asus2 fits

The plain chord on this root, A major, is the home chord (I) of the key of A major, the IV chord of E major and the V chord of D major. Asus2 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of A major, the three main chords are A, D and E, and the most used minor chord is F♯m. Try the movement Asus2, D, E, Asus2.
