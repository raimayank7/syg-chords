Adim chord (A diminished)

The A diminished chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
Adim
Notes
A, C, E♭
Formula
1 ♭3 ♭5
Semitones from root
0, 3, 6

A diminished chord, written Adim or with a small circle, is a minor chord with the fifth lowered by a semitone. Its notes are the root, the minor third and the diminished fifth. The two outer notes are three whole tones apart, an interval called the tritone.

It sounds tense and unstable, and is almost never a resting place. It occurs naturally on the seventh note of a major scale, and it is used as a passing chord, slipped between two chords a whole step apart to lead the ear from one to the other.

Adim on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 4 (D): fret 1. String 3 (G): fret 2. String 2 (B): fret 1. Play string 5 open. Do not play strings 6, 1.

A second way: movable shape on the four thin strings. String 4 (D): fret 7. String 3 (G): fret 8. String 2 (B): fret 10. String 1 (E): fret 8. Do not play strings 6, 5. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Play only the strings shown. The shape is small and movable: slide it up or down to change the root.

Adim on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 5. String 3 (C): fret 3. String 2 (E): fret 5. Play string 1 open. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

Adim on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: A, C, E♭. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: C, E♭, A; the second inversion: E♭, A, C. All are still Adim.

Play root, minor third and lowered fifth. Every note is three semitones from the next. Follow it with the major chord a semitone above the root and hear the tension release.

Where Adim fits

Adim is the chord built on the seventh note of the B♭ major scale (vii° in that key), and on the second note of G minor. It most often leads to B♭, a semitone above its root, or passes between A♭ and B♭m.
