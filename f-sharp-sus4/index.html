F♯sus4 chord (F♯ suspended fourth)

The F♯ suspended fourth chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
F♯sus4
Notes
F♯, B, C♯
Formula
1 4 5
Semitones from root
0, 5, 7

A suspended fourth chord, written F♯sus4, replaces the third with the fourth note of the scale, a semitone above the major third. Its notes are the root, the perfect fourth and the perfect fifth. It is the same chord as G♭sus4; the two names are used in different keys.

It creates a gentle tension that wants to settle downward on to the major chord. Church music has used this suspension for centuries, and it is everywhere in rock and pop as an embellishment on the last beat of a bar.

F♯sus4 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 2 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 4. String 4 (D): fret 4. String 3 (G): fret 4.

A second way: movable shape on the four thin strings. String 4 (D): fret 4. String 3 (G): fret 6. String 2 (B): fret 7. String 1 (E): fret 7. Do not play strings 6, 5. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

From an open major chord, add one finger (usually the little finger) one fret above the third. Add it and take it away in rhythm while strumming.

F♯sus4 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 4. String 3 (C): fret 1. String 2 (E): fret 2. String 1 (A): fret 2. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

F♯sus4 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: F♯, B, C♯. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: B, C♯, F♯; the second inversion: C♯, F♯, B. All are still F♯sus4.

Play root, fourth and fifth, then lower the fourth by a semitone to the third. Sus4 to major is one of the most satisfying two-chord movements there is.

Where F♯sus4 fits

The plain chord on this root, F♯ major, is the home chord (I) of the key of F♯ major, the IV chord of C♯ major and the V chord of B major. F♯sus4 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of F♯ major, the three main chords are F♯, B and C♯, and the most used minor chord is E♭m. Try the movement F♯sus4, B, C♯, F♯sus4.
