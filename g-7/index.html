G7 chord (G dominant seventh)

The G dominant seventh chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
G7
Notes
G, B, D, F
Formula
1 3 5 ♭7
Semitones from root
0, 4, 7, 10

A dominant seventh chord, written G7, is a major chord with a fourth note added: the minor seventh, a whole step below the root's octave. Its four notes are the root, major third, perfect fifth and minor seventh.

It sounds restless, as if it needs to move on. Its natural move is to the chord a fourth above (five semitones up): this pull from the "five" chord to the "one" chord is the strongest in Western harmony, and it drives blues, where every chord may be a seventh, as well as classical cadences and qawwali harmonium playing.

G7 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 6 (E): fret 3. String 5 (A): fret 2. String 1 (E): fret 1. Play strings 4, 3, 2 open.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 6th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 3 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 5. String 3 (G): fret 4. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Seventh chords are often easier than the major chords they come from, because the added note is made by lifting a finger or adding one on a thin string. In many common shapes the fifth of the chord is left out; the chord keeps its character without it.

G7 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 3 (C): fret 2. String 2 (E): fret 1. String 1 (A): fret 2. Play string 4 open. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

G7 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: G, B, D, F. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: B, D, F, G; the second inversion: D, F, G, B; the third inversion: F, G, B, D. All are still G7.

With four notes, use fingers 1, 2, 3 and 5. If the stretch is wide, leave out the fifth: root, third and seventh carry the sound.

Where G7 fits

G7 is the V7 chord of the key of C major and of C minor: it leads home to C or Cm. Play G7 and then C to hear the pull. In a twelve-bar blues in G, the three chords are G7, C7 and D7.
