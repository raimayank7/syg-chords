Dsus4 chord (D suspended fourth)

The D suspended fourth chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
Dsus4
Notes
D, G, A
Formula
1 4 5
Semitones from root
0, 5, 7

A suspended fourth chord, written Dsus4, replaces the third with the fourth note of the scale, a semitone above the major third. Its notes are the root, the perfect fourth and the perfect fifth.

It creates a gentle tension that wants to settle downward on to the major chord. Church music has used this suspension for centuries, and it is everywhere in rock and pop as an embellishment on the last beat of a bar.

Dsus4 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 3 (G): fret 2. String 2 (B): fret 3. String 1 (E): fret 3. Play string 4 open. Do not play strings 6, 5.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 5th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 5 to 1 at fret 5 (a barre). String 4 (D): fret 7. String 3 (G): fret 7. String 2 (B): fret 8. Do not play string 6. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

From an open major chord, add one finger (usually the little finger) one fret above the third. Add it and take it away in rhythm while strumming.

Dsus4 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 3 (C): fret 2. String 2 (E): fret 3. Play strings 4, 1 open.

Dsus4 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: D, G, A. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: G, A, D; the second inversion: A, D, G. All are still Dsus4.

Play root, fourth and fifth, then lower the fourth by a semitone to the third. Sus4 to major is one of the most satisfying two-chord movements there is.

Where Dsus4 fits

The plain chord on this root, D major, is the home chord (I) of the key of D major, the IV chord of A major and the V chord of G major. Dsus4 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of D major, the three main chords are D, G and A, and the most used minor chord is Bm. Try the movement Dsus4, G, A, Dsus4.
