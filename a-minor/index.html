Am chord (A minor)

The A minor chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
Am
Notes
A, C, E
Formula
1 ♭3 5
Semitones from root
0, 3, 7

A minor chord is a major chord with its middle note lowered by one semitone. Its three notes are the root, the minor third and the perfect fifth. It is written with a small m after the letter: Am.

That one lowered note changes the mood completely: minor chords sound darker, sadder or more serious than major ones. Songs in minor keys are built round them, and almost every song in a major key borrows two or three for contrast.

Am on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 4 (D): fret 2. String 3 (G): fret 2. String 2 (B): fret 1. Play strings 5, 1 open. Do not play string 6.

A second way: barre shape with the root on the 6th string. Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 5 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 7. String 4 (D): fret 7. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Compare this shape with the major chord on the same root: usually only one finger moves, by one fret. Practise switching between the two to hear the third change.

Am on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 2. Play strings 3, 2, 1 open. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

Am on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: A, C, E. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: C, E, A; the second inversion: E, A, C. All are still Am.

Play root, minor third and fifth with fingers 1, 3 and 5. Play the major chord, then lower only the middle note: that is the whole difference.

Where Am fits

A minor is the home chord of the key of A minor. It also belongs to three major keys: it is the ii chord of G major, the iii chord of F major and the vi chord of C major.

In A minor it is usually joined by Dm and Em, and by the major chords C, F and G. Try Am, F, C, G.
