A♭5 chord (A♭ power chord (fifth))

The A♭ power chord (fifth) chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
A♭5
Notes
A♭, E♭
Formula
1 5
Semitones from root
0, 7

A power chord, written A♭5, has only two different notes: the root and the perfect fifth, usually with the root repeated an octave higher. Strictly it is an interval, not a chord, since a chord needs three notes. It is the same chord as G♯5; the two names are used in different keys.

Without a third it is neither major nor minor, so it fits over either. On an electric guitar with distortion it sounds clear and strong where a full chord would turn to mud, which made it the foundation of rock, punk and metal. On an acoustic guitar it gives a lean, driving accompaniment.

A♭5 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): String 6 (E): fret 4. String 5 (A): fret 6. String 4 (D): fret 6. Do not play strings 3, 2, 1.

Play only the two or three thickest strings of the shape and mute the rest with the side of your fretting hand. The shape slides up and down the neck unchanged: learn it once and you have every power chord.

A♭5 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): Lay your first finger flat across strings 4 to 1 at fret 8 (a barre). String 2 (E): fret 11. String 1 (A): fret 11.

A♭5 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: A♭, E♭. With only two different notes, the root is usually doubled an octave higher.

Play root and fifth in the left hand, or root, fifth and octave. This "open fifth" is the usual left-hand bass under right-hand chords.

Where A♭5 fits

The plain chord on this root, A♭ major, is the home chord (I) of the key of A♭ major, the IV chord of E♭ major and the V chord of C♯ major. A♭5 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of A♭ major, the three main chords are A♭, C♯ and E♭, and the most used minor chord is Fm. Try the movement A♭5, C♯, E♭, A♭5.
