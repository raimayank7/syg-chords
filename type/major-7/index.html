Major seventh chords

The major seventh chord on all twelve roots, for guitar, ukulele and keyboard. Formula: 1 3 5 7.

What a major seventh chord is

A major seventh chord, written Cmaj7, adds the major seventh to a major chord: the note one semitone below the root's octave. Its notes are the root, major third, perfect fifth and major seventh.

It sounds soft, warm and a little dreamy, without the restlessness of the dominant seventh. It is a staple of jazz, bossa nova, soul and romantic film songs, usually on the first or fourth chord of a major key.

On guitar

Do not confuse it with the plain seventh chord: Cmaj7 and the dominant seventh on the same root differ by one fret on one string, and they do very different jobs. Let the chord ring; it rewards a gentle strum or fingerpicking.

On keyboard

The seventh sits right next to the root's octave, so root and seventh clash if played side by side. Keep the root at the bottom and the seventh at the top, or play the root alone in the left hand.
