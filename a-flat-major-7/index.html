A♭maj7 chord (A♭ major seventh)

The A♭ major seventh chord on guitar, ukulele and keyboard, with its notes, formula and the keys it belongs to.

Symbol
A♭maj7
Notes
A♭, C, E♭, G
Formula
1 3 5 7
Semitones from root
0, 4, 7, 11

A major seventh chord, written A♭maj7, adds the major seventh to a major chord: the note one semitone below the root's octave. Its notes are the root, major third, perfect fifth and major seventh. It is the same chord as G♯maj7; the two names are used in different keys.

It sounds soft, warm and a little dreamy, without the restlessness of the dominant seventh. It is a staple of jazz, bossa nova, soul and romantic film songs, usually on the first or fourth chord of a major key.

A♭maj7 on guitar

The first shape, in words (strings are numbered from 6, the thickest, to 1, the thinnest): Lay your first finger flat across strings 6 to 1 at fret 4 (a barre). String 5 (A): fret 6. String 4 (D): fret 5. String 3 (G): fret 5.

A second way: movable shape on the four thin strings. String 4 (D): fret 6. String 3 (G): fret 8. String 2 (B): fret 8. String 1 (E): fret 8. Do not play strings 6, 5. It contains the same notes higher or lower on the neck; use whichever lies nearer to the chords around it.

Do not confuse it with the plain seventh chord: A♭maj7 and the dominant seventh on the same root differ by one fret on one string, and they do very different jobs. Let the chord ring; it rewards a gentle strum or fingerpicking.

A♭maj7 on ukulele

In standard G, C, E, A tuning (strings numbered from 4, the G string nearest your face, to 1, the A string): String 4 (G): fret 1. String 3 (C): fret 3. String 2 (E): fret 3. String 1 (A): fret 3. On a ukulele the lowest-sounding note of a chord is not always its root, because the fourth string is tuned high; the chord is the same.

A♭maj7 on piano and keyboard

Root position, from the bottom: A♭, C, E♭, G. The same notes rearranged give the first inversion: C, E♭, G, A♭; the second inversion: E♭, G, A♭, C; the third inversion: G, A♭, C, E♭. All are still A♭maj7.

The seventh sits right next to the root's octave, so root and seventh clash if played side by side. Keep the root at the bottom and the seventh at the top, or play the root alone in the left hand.

Where A♭maj7 fits

The plain chord on this root, A♭ major, is the home chord (I) of the key of A♭ major, the IV chord of E♭ major and the V chord of C♯ major. A♭maj7 is used in the same places, as a colour or decoration of the plain chord.

In the key of A♭ major, the three main chords are A♭, C♯ and E♭, and the most used minor chord is Fm. Try the movement A♭maj7, C♯, E♭, A♭maj7.
