Five-string banjo: open G tuning

The usual tuning of the five-string banjo is called open G, because the open strings together sound a G major chord. The notes are G, D, G, B, D, written with a small g first: g D G B D. The small g is the short fifth string, the one whose peg sits part of the way up the neck. Although it is nearest your face, it is the highest string, tuned to the G above middle C.

How to tune

  1. Tap Start tuner.
  2. Pick the 4th string, the lowest, and tune it to D.
  3. Tune the 3rd string to G, the 2nd to B and the 1st to D.
  4. Last, tune the short 5th string to the high G. It should sound the same note as the 1st string pressed at the 5th fret.
  5. Strum the open strings: you should hear a bright G chord. Check each string again.

Why the banjo drifts

A banjo's "top" is a stretched skin or plastic head, like a drum, and the bridge simply stands on it, held by the strings. The head moves with temperature and humidity, and with it the tuning. Banjo players tune often, and joke about it. If the bridge is knocked out of place, the open strings can be tuned perfectly and the fretted notes will still be wrong; the bridge should stand so that the 12th-fret note of each string is exactly an octave above the open string, which you can check with this tuner.

Four-string banjos

The tenor banjo has four strings and is most often tuned in fifths, C, G, D, A, like a viola; Irish players commonly tune it lower, G, D, A, E. The plectrum banjo is usually C, G, B, D. The viola tuner matches the standard tenor tuning.

Tips for a steady reading

  • The banjo is loud and bright; hold the phone a little further away than for a guitar.
  • Pick one string and lightly rest your hand on the others, so that they do not ring.
  • Tune up to each note from below.
