Violin tuning

The four strings of a violin are tuned, from lowest to highest, G, D, A, E. Each string is a fifth above the one before it, which is the distance from "do" to "sol". The A string sounds the A above middle C, the note orchestras tune to.

Pegs and fine tuners

A violin has two ways of tuning each string. The pegs in the scroll make large changes. The fine tuners, small screws on the tailpiece, make small ones. Student violins usually have a fine tuner on every string; others have one only on the E.

  • If the note is only slightly out, use the fine tuner: turning it clockwise tightens the string and raises the pitch.
  • If the fine tuner is screwed right down or the string is far out, loosen the fine tuner most of the way, then use the peg.
  • To use a peg, turn it a very small amount while pressing it gently inwards, towards the pegbox, so that it grips. Pegs hold by friction; a peg that is not pushed in will slip back.

Take care: strings and bridges

Violin strings, the E above all, break if they are tightened much beyond their note. Turn pegs in tiny steps, pluck after each step, and stop as soon as you reach the note. Always tune up to a note from below. As you tighten strings, the top of the bridge is pulled slowly towards the fingerboard; glance at it now and then to see that it still stands upright. If you are a beginner, ask your teacher to show you peg tuning before you try it alone.

How to tune with this page

  1. Rest the violin on your knee like a small guitar, or hold it under your chin.
  2. Tap Start tuner.
  3. Pluck the A string (second from the right as you look at the front), or bow it with long, steady strokes.
  4. Adjust until the display reads "In tune".
  5. Then tune D, then G, then E.
  6. Check all four again.

Bowing gives a steadier reading than plucking, because the note does not die away. Press evenly; a heavy bow pushes the pitch slightly sharp.

Tuning in fifths by ear

Experienced players tune only the A to a reference and then tune the other strings to it by bowing two neighbouring strings together and listening. When a fifth is perfectly in tune, the two notes blend and the slow "wah-wah" pulsing between them stops. You can practise this with the reference tone buttons under the tuner. See tuning by ear.

In Indian classical music

Violinists in Carnatic and Hindustani music tune differently, to the singer's or their own tonic: commonly the strings are set to Pa, Sa, Pa, Sa (low to high) in the chosen pitch. For that, choose your Sa in the tuner's "Show notes as" box on the find your Sa page; the tuner will then name notes as swaras.
