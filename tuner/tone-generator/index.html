How to use the tone generator

Choose a note and an octave and tap Play. A steady tone sounds for up to thirty seconds, or until you tap again. Change the note while it is playing and the tone follows. The row of buttons plays the twelve notes of the octave you have chosen, like a small keyboard. The frequency is shown in hertz, worked out from A4 = 440 Hz or from the reference you have set on the home page tuner.

What it is for

  • Tuning by ear. Play the note your string should have and bring the string to it, listening for the slow pulsing that disappears when the two agree. See how to tune by ear.
  • A drone for practice. Singers and instrumentalists practise scales against a held note to train intonation. Set your Sa on the find your Sa page and the notes are named as swaras.
  • Finding a starting note. A choir leader or a singer without an instrument can take the first note of a song from the phone.
  • Checking an instrument without a microphone, for example in a noisy room where the tuner cannot hear.
  • Lessons in sound. Play A3, A4 and A5 one after another to hear octaves; play two notes a semitone apart on two phones and listen to the beats.

Octave numbers

Each octave runs from C up to B and has a number. C4 is middle C, the C nearest the middle of a piano, and A4 is the A above it, 440 Hz, the standard tuning note. The open strings of a guitar run from E2 to E4; a violin's from G3 to E5. The note frequency chart lists every note.

Why low notes sound weak on a phone

The tiny speaker of a phone cannot move enough air to reproduce low frequencies. Notes in octaves 1 and 2, such as the strings of a bass guitar, come out thin, buzzy or silent, although the phone is producing them correctly. Earphones or a larger speaker will play them properly. Likewise, the highest octaves may be unpleasantly piercing; keep the volume moderate.

The kind of tone

The generator plays a soft "triangle" wave: a clear tone with a few gentle overtones, easier to hear on small speakers than a pure sine wave and easier to tune against than a harsh buzz. It starts and stops with a short fade so that there is no click.

Protect your ears

A steady tone, especially a high one, is more tiring than music at the same volume. Keep the volume low, particularly with earphones, and do not leave a tone playing for long periods.

Accuracy

The tone's pitch is set by your device's audio clock, which is far more exact than any ear needs. Bluetooth speakers and earphones add a small delay but do not change the pitch.
