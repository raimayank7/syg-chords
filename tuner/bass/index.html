Standard bass tuning

A four-string bass guitar is tuned E, A, D, G from the thickest string to the thinnest. These are the same notes as the four lowest strings of a guitar, one octave lower. A five-string bass usually adds a low B below the E.

Tuning a bass with a microphone

Bass notes are very low: the open E string vibrates only about 41 times a second. That creates two difficulties. The small microphones and speakers of phones and laptops hear and play low notes poorly, and a low note needs a longer listen before its pitch is certain. A few habits make it work:

  • Use an amplifier, and hold the phone near the speaker, or tune an acoustic bass close to the microphone.
  • Pluck gently with the pad of the finger, over the end of the fretboard, and let the note ring for two or three seconds before you read the needle. The first instant of a plucked string is sharp and settles as it rings.
  • Tune with harmonics. Touch the string lightly directly over the 12th fret and pluck: the bell-like note that sounds is exactly one octave above the open string, and the tuner hears it far more easily. Tune the harmonic to the same letter.
  • Mute the other strings with your hand, so that they do not ring in sympathy and confuse the reading.

The reference tones

The buttons under the tuner play the note of each string. A phone speaker cannot reproduce the lowest of them properly, so you may hear a thin buzz or nothing. Use earphones, or tune the 12th-fret harmonic against a tone an octave higher from the tone generator.

Step by step

  1. Start with the E string (or the B on a five-string).
  2. Bring it up to pitch from below.
  3. Move to A, D and G.
  4. Check all the strings again; a bass neck is long and responds to changes in tension.

Why bass tuning matters so much

The bass plays the lowest note of the harmony. If it is a little flat, every chord in the band sounds wrong, even when the guitars are perfectly tuned. Bass strings are heavy and hold their tuning well, but changes of temperature, especially moving from a cold room to a warm stage, shift them. Check before every session.

Common variations

  • Drop D: lower the E string by two frets' worth to D.
  • Half a step down: E flat, A flat, D flat, G flat, to match guitars tuned down.
  • Six-string bass: usually B, E, A, D, G, C.
