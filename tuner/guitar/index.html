Standard guitar tuning

A six-string guitar in standard tuning is tuned, from the thickest string to the thinnest, to E, A, D, G, B, E. The strings are numbered from the thinnest: the high E is the 1st string and the low E is the 6th. A sentence such as "Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie" helps beginners remember the order from thick to thin. The same tuning is used on acoustic, classical and electric guitars.

How to tune with this page

  1. Sit somewhere quiet. Put the phone or laptop about an arm's length from the guitar.
  2. Tap Start tuner and allow the microphone when the browser asks.
  3. Pluck the 6th string (the thickest) once and let it ring. Do not strum.
  4. The display names the nearest string and shows a needle. To the left means flat (too low): tighten the string. To the right means sharp (too high): loosen it.
  5. Turn the tuning peg slowly while the string rings, until the needle sits in the middle and the display says "In tune".
  6. Repeat for the 5th, 4th, 3rd, 2nd and 1st strings.
  7. Go round all six once more. Tightening one string slightly changes the pull on the neck and can move the others.

Which way do I turn the peg?

On most guitars, turning a peg so that the string winds further on to its post tightens the string and raises the pitch. If you are unsure, pluck the string, turn a little and listen: a rising sound means you are tightening. Always check that you are turning the peg of the string you are plucking; following the string up to its post with a finger avoids the commonest beginner's accident, a string wound until it snaps.

Tune up, not down

If a string is sharp, loosen it until it is a little flat and then tighten back up to the note. A string brought up to pitch holds its tuning better than one let down to it, because the slack in the gear and at the nut has been taken up.

If the tuner names the wrong string

A string that is far out of tune may be nearer to its neighbour's note, and the tuner will then show the neighbour. Tap the string's button under the tuner to hear the note it should have, and bring the string roughly to that sound by ear first. New strings, and guitars that have not been played for months, often need this.

New strings will not stay in tune

New strings stretch for the first day or two. Tune, play for a few minutes, and tune again; repeat several times. Gently pulling each new string away from the fretboard a little, then retuning, speeds this up. Nylon strings on a classical guitar take longest to settle.
