Alternate guitar tunings

Standard tuning, E A D G B E, is a compromise that makes most chords and scales reasonably easy. Change one or more strings and the guitar becomes a different instrument: new chords fall under the fingers, open strings ring where they did not, and slide playing becomes possible. Here are ten tunings in common use. For each, the strings are listed from the thickest (6th) to the thinnest (1st), with a button that plays the note of every string so that you can tune by ear. To tune with the microphone, use the chromatic tuner, which names whatever note it hears.

Standard

The reference for everything else.

Drop D

Only the 6th string changes: lower it by a whole step. Gives a deep D and one-finger power chords on the three thickest strings. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 2 semitones to D.

Half step down

Every string one fret lower. Easier on the voice and the fingers; many rock songs use it. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 1 semitone to D♯; 5th string down by 1 semitone to G♯; 4th string down by 1 semitone to C♯; 3rd string down by 1 semitone to F♯; 2nd string down by 1 semitone to A♯; 1st string down by 1 semitone to D♯.

Whole step down (D standard)

Every string two frets lower. Heavier sound; strings feel slack unless they are thick. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 2 semitones to D; 5th string down by 2 semitones to G; 4th string down by 2 semitones to C; 3rd string down by 2 semitones to F; 2nd string down by 2 semitones to A; 1st string down by 2 semitones to D.

Double drop D

Both E strings lowered to D. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 2 semitones to D; 1st string down by 2 semitones to D.

DADGAD

Neither major nor minor when strummed open. Loved for folk and fingerstyle playing. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 2 semitones to D; 2nd string down by 2 semitones to A; 1st string down by 2 semitones to D.

Open G

The open strings sound a G major chord. A favourite for slide guitar. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 2 semitones to D; 5th string down by 2 semitones to G; 1st string down by 2 semitones to D.

Open D

The open strings sound a D major chord. Also used for slide. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 2 semitones to D; 3rd string down by 1 semitone to F♯; 2nd string down by 2 semitones to A; 1st string down by 2 semitones to D.

Open E

A whole step above open D: three strings are tightened, so go carefully. From standard tuning: 5th string up by 2 semitones to B; 4th string up by 2 semitones to E; 3rd string up by 1 semitone to G♯.

Open C

A deep, ringing C major chord. Several strings move a long way. From standard tuning: 6th string down by 4 semitones to C; 5th string down by 2 semitones to G; 4th string down by 2 semitones to C; 2nd string up by 1 semitone to C.

How to change tuning safely

  • Lowering a string is always safe. Raising one adds tension: go slowly, and do not take a string more than about a whole step above its standard note. For open E, many players prefer to tune to open D and put a capo on the 2nd fret.
  • Retune all the strings twice. Changing the tension of some strings bends the neck slightly and shifts the others.
  • Tune up to each note, coming from below.
  • Low tunings feel slack and may buzz. Thicker strings help if you stay in a low tuning.
  • Strings wear faster if they are retuned many times a day; the metal tires at the post and the nut.

What changes when you retune

Every chord shape you know moves. In drop D only the 6th string changes, so shapes on the top five strings still work. In an open tuning, laying one finger flat across all six strings at any fret gives a major chord: the 5th fret gives the chord a fourth above the open one, the 7th fret a fifth above. That is why open tunings suit slide playing and why beginners can make music in them quickly.

Capo or retune?

A capo raises all strings by the same amount without changing their relationships, so every chord shape stays as you learnt it and only the key changes. Retuning changes the relationships between strings, and with them the sound and the shapes. To sing a song higher, use a capo. To get a new sound, retune.
