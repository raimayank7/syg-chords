Viola tuning

The viola's four strings are tuned, from lowest to highest, C, G, D, A. Like the violin's, they are a fifth apart. The viola shares three of its notes with the violin (G, D and A) and adds a C below instead of the violin's E above. Its lowest string is the C below middle C. The same four notes, one octave lower, are the strings of the cello.

How to tune

  1. Tap Start tuner and allow the microphone.
  2. Play the open A string with a long, even bow stroke, or pluck it.
  3. Use the fine tuner for small corrections and the peg for large ones, pushing the peg gently inwards as you turn so that it holds.
  4. Tune D, then G, then C.
  5. Check all four once more.

The low C string

The C string is thick and slow to speak. Phone microphones hear it less well than the upper strings, so give it time: bow it steadily for two or three seconds near the fingerboard, and read the needle only when it has stopped wandering. If the reading jumps between C and the G above, the microphone is catching the string's overtones; play more softly and bring the device closer.

Tune up, in small steps

As with all bowed instruments, approach each note from below, and turn pegs by very small amounts. Strings that are taken well above their pitch can break, and the upper strings are the most at risk. Watch that the bridge stays upright as the strings tighten. Beginners should learn peg tuning from a teacher.

Checking by ear

When you can, bow two neighbouring strings together. A true fifth sounds open and steady; a fifth that is slightly out pulses slowly. Removing that pulse by ear is how violists normally finish their tuning. The reference tone buttons let you compare one string at a time. More in tuning by ear.

Why the viola reads a different clef

Viola music is written mostly in the alto clef, in which middle C sits on the middle line. This is only a matter of notation: it places the viola's range neatly on the staff. The tuner does not mind which clef you read; it shows letter names and octave numbers. On this site C4 means middle C, so the viola's open strings are C3, G3, D4 and A4.

Strings and humidity

Wooden pegs swell in damp weather and shrink in dry weather, so an instrument that held its tuning in the monsoon may slip in winter, and the other way round. Keep the viola in its case away from heaters, coolers and direct sun, and check the tuning every time you play.
