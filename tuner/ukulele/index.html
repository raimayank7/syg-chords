Standard ukulele tuning

Soprano, concert and tenor ukuleles are tuned G, C, E, A, counting from the string nearest your face (the 4th) to the one nearest the floor (the 1st). Many players remember it with the phrase "Good Children Eat Apples", or by singing "My dog has fleas" on the four open strings.

The high G surprises beginners

On most ukuleles the 4th string, G, is tuned higher than the C string next to it, not lower. This is called re-entrant tuning, and it gives the ukulele its bright, close sound. So the lowest note of the instrument is the C of the 3rd string. If you are used to a guitar, do not try to tune the G down an octave: it will be far too slack. (Some tenor players fit a special thicker "low G" string; if you have one, tune it to the G below middle C.)

How to tune

  1. Hold the ukulele as you would to play. Tap Start tuner.
  2. Pluck the 4th string with your thumb and let it ring.
  3. If the needle is to the left, the string is low: tighten its peg. If to the right, loosen.
  4. When it reads "In tune", move to the 3rd, 2nd and 1st strings.
  5. Strum all four. Then check each again.

Ukuleles go out of tune quickly

Ukulele strings are made of nylon or similar soft materials, which stretch far more than steel. A new ukulele, or one with new strings, may need tuning every few minutes for the first week. This is normal and not a fault. Tune, play, tune again; the strings settle. Even after that, check the tuning each time you pick the instrument up, since nylon responds to warmth and damp.

Friction pegs and geared pegs

Some ukuleles have pegs that stick straight out behind the headstock and hold by friction alone. If such a peg slips, tighten the small screw at its end a quarter turn. Geared pegs, which stick out sideways like a guitar's, turn more slowly and are easier for fine tuning.

Baritone ukulele

The larger baritone ukulele is tuned differently: D, G, B, E, the same as the four thinnest strings of a guitar. For that, use the chromatic tuner on the home page or the guitar tuner, which will recognise those four strings.

Other tunings

An older tuning, a whole step higher at A, D, F sharp, B, is sometimes found in old song books, and some players lower the instrument a half step to suit a singer. The chromatic tuner shows any of these.
