This table gives the frequency, in hertz (vibrations per second), of every note in nine octaves, in the tuning system used by almost all modern instruments: twelve equal semitones to the octave, with the A above middle C set at 440 Hz. Rows are the twelve notes; columns are octave numbers. Middle C is C4. Read across a row and each figure is double the one before: that is what an octave is.

The rule behind the table

Start from A4 = 440. To go up one semitone, multiply by the twelfth root of 2, about 1.059463; to go down, divide. Twelve steps up multiply the frequency by exactly 2. As a formula, the frequency of the note n semitones above A4 is 440 times 2 to the power n/12 (use a negative n for notes below). For example, middle C is nine semitones below A4: 440 divided by 2 to the power 9/12 gives 261.63 Hz.

Sharps and flats

The table uses sharps. Each sharp has a second name as a flat: C♯ is also D♭, D♯ is E♭, F♯ is G♭, G♯ is A♭ and A♯ is B♭. In equal temperament the two names mean exactly the same frequency.

Sargam

In Indian music the same twelve notes are named from a movable Sa. With Sa on C the notes in the table read, in order: Sa, komal Re, Re, komal Ga, Ga, Ma, tivra Ma, Pa, komal Dha, Dha, komal Ni, Ni. Choose another Sa and the names shift with it. See find your Sa.

What the numbers mean in practice

  • Low notes are close together in hertz. E1 and F1 are about 2.5 Hz apart; E6 and F6 are about 78 Hz apart. A tuner therefore has to listen longer to be sure of a low note.
  • Hearing range. A young person with good hearing can hear from roughly 20 Hz to roughly 20,000 Hz, so the lowest row of octave 0 is at the edge of hearing, felt as much as heard.
  • Other reference pitches. If an orchestra tunes to A = 442, every figure is higher by the same proportion, about 0.45 percent. Our tuner can be set to another reference.
  • Real instruments stray a little. Pianos are tuned slightly wide at the top and bottom, and singers and string players adjust notes by ear to the harmony. The table is the agreed grid, not a law.
