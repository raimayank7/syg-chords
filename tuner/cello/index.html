Cello tuning

The cello is tuned C, G, D, A from the lowest string to the highest, in fifths. These are the notes of the viola one octave lower. The top string is the A below middle C, and the bottom string is the C two octaves below middle C.

How to tune

  1. Sit in playing position with the cello steady on its endpin. Put the phone on a music stand or chair close to the instrument.
  2. Tap Start tuner.
  3. Bow the open A string with slow, full strokes.
  4. Most cellos have a fine tuner for each string on the tailpiece: use these for small changes. For large changes use the pegs, a tiny turn at a time, pushing the peg in as you turn.
  5. Tune D, G and C in the same way, then check all four again.

Getting a reading on the low strings

The open C string vibrates about 65 times a second, lower than phone microphones are designed to capture well. Help the tuner:

  • Bow, do not pluck. A bowed note is steady and long.
  • Bow fairly near the bridge with a firm, even stroke; it brings out a clearer tone.
  • Bring the device close, on the side of the low strings.
  • If the needle will not settle, play the harmonic instead: touch the string lightly at its midpoint and bow. The note that sounds is one octave higher, with the same letter name, and is much easier to hear.

Tune gently

Cello pegs are large and a small turn changes the pitch a great deal. Hold the neck steady with one hand, or brace the cello against your body, while turning a peg with the other. Always come up to the note from below. Check that the bridge has not been pulled forward as the strings tightened; it should stand at a right angle to the top of the instrument.

Listening for fifths

Cellists tune the A to a reference note and then check each pair of neighbouring strings together. When the fifth is true, the sound is calm; when it is not, you hear a slow beat. On the low strings the beats are slow and easy to hear, which makes the cello a good instrument on which to learn the skill. See tuning by ear.

Looking after the tuning

  • Keep the cello away from heaters, coolers and direct sunlight; wood and gut or synthetic strings respond to temperature and damp.
  • New strings stretch for several days and need frequent retuning.
  • If a peg slips or sticks, a violin maker can fit it properly; do not force it.

Related tuners: viola and violin. The reference tones for very low notes are faint on a phone speaker; use earphones.
